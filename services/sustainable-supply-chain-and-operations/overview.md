# Purpose

How can impacts, risk, cost, and volatility be reduced by changing what is
bought, who it is bought from, how it is produced, moved, processed, used,
or recovered?

# Primary Decision Owners

* Procurement and sourcing leaders
* Supply chain and operations executives
* Category management teams
* Logistics and transportation leaders
* Manufacturing and processing operations

# Core Decision

Which operational and upstream supply chain changes will most effectively reduce
environmental impact, cost, risk, and volatility — and how do we implement them?

# Food x Climate Scope Boundary

This service family applies to improvements inside company operations or upstream
supply chains.

**What belongs here:**
- Supplier engagement, sourcing policy, and responsible procurement
- Supply chain sustainability measurement and target-setting at the operational level
- Logistics optimization, waste reduction, and lean operations
- Digital supply chain strategy, traceability, and transparency tools
- Circular economy, food waste, and resource recovery within operations
- Supplier resilience, contingency planning, and category management

**What does not belong here:**
- Enterprise-level Scope 3 target-setting and disclosure (see Corporate Sustainability)
- Environmental asset or credit market participation (see Carbon & Ecosystem Services)
- Corporate strategy and market entry decisions (see Strategy & Innovation)

**Note on overlap with Corporate Sustainability:** Corporate Sustainability also
contains a Supply Chain & Operations capability block. This is intentional.
The governing distinction is the decision owner: this service family is owned by
procurement, sourcing, and operations teams. The Corporate Sustainability version
is owned by CSOs and ESG teams who are driving supply chain change through the
enterprise sustainability program framework.
