# Regenerative Agriculture — Strategic Topic Page Brief

> Owner approved this document as the repository first draft on August 3, 2026.
> Publication approval has not been granted.

## Owner-approved first-draft direction

### Position in the value chain

**Primary value-chain node:** Agricultural Production

Land-based crop production, with Inputs and Commodity Trading as important adjacent nodes.

### Role of this Area of Expertise

Focuses on changing how annual and perennial crops are produced so that farm performance, environmental outcomes, resilience, and downstream value can improve.

Cultivation to Claim is not the stage structure for this page. It is the
Terra Nexus integration framework used to connect performance improvements
with the most valuable supportable claims and market mechanisms across the
participants needed to deliver them.

### Topic boundary

Regenerative Agriculture covers annual and perennial crop-production systems on
agricultural land, including row crops, specialty crops, and permanent crops.

- Regenerative Rangeland covers grazing systems.
- Agroforestry covers systems where trees are an intentional production component.
- Aquaculture covers production below water.
- Biodiversity & Ecosystem Resilience addresses broader ecological and community context.
- Sustainable Supply Chains connects production to procurement, movement, processing,
  sale, delivery, and claims.

### Geographic positioning

The page is globally relevant. U.S. examples, programs, and market mechanisms
may be used where they are most relevant, but the topic should not be presented
as U.S.-only.

## Page purpose

Position Terra Nexus as the independent integration partner that helps clients
turn regenerative agriculture from a field-level ambition into a commercially
viable, operationally executable, measurable, and credible value-chain program.

The page should not be a generic explanation of farming practices. It should
show how agronomic change, producer economics, commodity origination, technology,
environmental accounting, program operations, and claims must work together.

## Primary audience

### Commodity Traders and Originators

Commodity traders are the primary audience because they frequently sit between
producers and downstream buyers and can connect:

- producer relationships;
- agricultural practices and evidence;
- physical commodity origination and aggregation;
- customer demand and commercial contracts;
- environmental attributes and value allocation;
- traceability, chain of custody, and claims;
- program execution across multiple geographies and crops.

The page should show traders as potential program builders, aggregators,
commercial intermediaries, data coordinators, and trusted links between the farm
and the buyer.

## Working market thesis

> Regenerative agriculture creates durable value when field-level change,
> producer economics, commodity sourcing, measurement, commercial incentives,
> program operations, and credible claims are designed to work together.

A practice list alone does not create a scalable program. The commercial and
operating model must answer:

- where the opportunity exists;
- which producers and supply sheds can participate;
- which practices or outcomes are appropriate;
- how transition costs and risks are shared;
- what data and evidence are required;
- how physical commodities and environmental attributes move;
- how impacts are quantified and accounted for;
- which claims can be supported;
- how the program will be managed and independently reviewed.

## From performance improvement to shared value

The Area of Expertise identifies the domain in which performance can be improved.
It does not prescribe one intervention, one program, or one claim.

Terra Nexus helps clients:

1. identify where performance can be improved across the relevant value-chain nodes;
2. determine which fit-for-purpose changes are practical without publishing a universal intervention playbook;
3. define the evidence, data, traceability, operating, and collaboration requirements;
4. evaluate the claims and market mechanisms that the improved performance may support;
5. compare participant-specific value, costs, risks, marketability, utilization, and stacking constraints; and
6. design and manage the highest-value supportable play across the participants required to deliver it.

The same underlying performance improvement may support different claims with
different levels of rigor, cost, commercial reach, and value. Some claims remain
within a specific value chain, some qualify participation or support marketing,
and some create transferable financial assets. The best pathway is therefore not
always the claim with the highest nominal price.

The public page should explain this decision problem without publishing Terra
Nexus's detailed intervention libraries, claim-stacking logic, pricing models, or
client-specific commercialization pathways.

## Decisions clients face

### Strategy and P&L owners

- Which regenerative agriculture opportunities fit the business strategy?
- Which crops, supply sheds, customers, and outcomes offer a credible business case?
- Should the company build, buy, partner, or participate in an existing program?
- How should commercial value, transition costs, and risk be allocated?

### Origination, merchandising, and procurement

- Which producers and suppliers are ready to participate?
- How should regenerative volumes be sourced, aggregated, priced, and contracted?
- How should existing adopters, new practice adoption, and incomplete traceability be handled?
- What evidence must move with the commodity or environmental attribute?

### Sustainability and environmental-market teams

- Is the program intended for Scope 3, insetting, carbon credits, product claims,
  resilience, biodiversity, water, or a combination?
- Which accounting, target-setting, market, or certification framework applies?
- How should outcomes, removals, reductions, and claims be separated?
- What can be reported credibly and at what level of assurance?

### Program, operations, and technology teams

- Which data must be collected directly and which can be modeled or remotely sensed?
- How will systems, vendors, models, field evidence, and ledgers connect?
- How will data quality, changes, exceptions, and versioning be managed?
- What activities should be internal, outsourced, or delivered as a managed service?

### Finance, investment, and risk teams

- What is the cost per acre, tonne, supplier, or unit of product?
- How durable are projected outcomes and commercial returns?
- What methodology, counterparty, operational, fraud, and claims risks exist?
- Is a program, project, platform, or portfolio investment supportable?

## Guardrails

- Do not prescribe a universal definition or practice list.
- Practice recommendations depend on crop, geography, production system,
  agronomic context, and program requirements.
- Do not guarantee environmental outcomes, credit issuance, Scope 3 recognition,
  premiums, certification, or claim acceptance.
- Do not imply that Terra Nexus performs accredited validation or verification.
- Develop specialized agronomic recommendations with qualified agronomy and
  technical partners.
- Leave legal, tax, certification, accounting, and investment opinions to the
  appropriate qualified parties.
- Do not imply that a technology platform, model, certification, or methodology
  is universally appropriate.
- Do not present illustrative companies as clients.
