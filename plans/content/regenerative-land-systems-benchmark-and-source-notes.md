# Regenerative Land Systems — Website Benchmark and Source Notes

**Status:** Internal research memo  
**Research date:** August 4, 2026  
**Purpose:** Support expanded Regenerative Agriculture and Regenerative Rangeland website pages and establish a research pattern for the remaining Areas of Expertise.

## Research question

How can Terra Nexus explain regenerative agriculture and rangeland in enough depth to be useful while differentiating from a producer program, carbon developer, software platform, certification body, or generic consulting firm?

## Key conclusion

The strongest market pages typically do one or more of the following well:

1. explain why the topic matters;
2. translate the topic into tangible outcomes;
3. show a simple program or participation journey;
4. speak separately to different audiences;
5. connect land-management change to evidence and market value;
6. show proof, scale, partners, or technical credibility.

Most peer pages are designed around one specific business model. Terra Nexus's opportunity is to explain how the complete system must work and where an independent advisor and program manager can help different participants design, diligence, build, and manage that system.

## Peer-page observations

### Cargill — Regenerative Agriculture

**What the page does well**

- Leads with farmer and supply-chain resilience rather than carbon alone.
- States that every farm is different and practices should fit location, crops, and business model.
- Gives visitors understandable examples such as cover crops, reduced tillage, nutrient optimization, grazing management, and agroforestry.
- Connects farmer adoption with customers, market access, agronomic support, and environmental programs.
- Uses program examples to show how a trader can connect producers and downstream demand.

**What Terra Nexus should adapt—not copy**

- Explain representative practices while preserving the fit-for-purpose guardrail.
- Show how producers, originators, customers, data, incentives, and claims connect.
- Make the commercial and operating system visible.

**What does not transfer directly**

- Cargill is a physical trader and program operator with its own producer programs.
- Terra Nexus should not imply physical origination, farmer enrollment capacity, or ownership of a comparable program unless an approved record supports it.

### Cargill — Rotational Grazing

**What the page does well**

- Defines rotational grazing in simple terms.
- Explains rest and recovery as the core concept.
- Organizes benefits across soil, water, biodiversity, livestock, and climate.
- Uses an educational article to help non-specialist visitors understand the practice.

**What Terra Nexus should adapt—not copy**

- Include a clear explanation of rotational and prescribed grazing.
- Place the practice inside a broader adaptive management system.
- Qualify outcomes and avoid making one practice the definition of regenerative rangeland.

### Grassroots Carbon — Rancher Pathway

**What the page does well**

- Speaks directly to rancher economics and operating concerns.
- Separates rancher and corporate journeys.
- Explains a simple participation process: apply, meet, measure, manage, and receive payment.
- Shows that tools, education, measurements, and financial support are part of implementation.
- Makes the producer proposition tangible.

**What Terra Nexus should adapt—not copy**

- Explain producer participation, transition costs, infrastructure, evidence, and incentives.
- Show a high-level program lifecycle.
- Use audience-specific sections within the topic page.

**What does not transfer directly**

- Grassroots Carbon sells a specific soil-carbon program and makes program-specific payment, scale, and outcome claims.
- Terra Nexus should not imply that carbon credits are always the preferred pathway or adopt Grassroots Carbon's claims or program steps as its own.

### Grassroots Carbon — Corporate Pathway

**What the page does well**

- Connects a land-based project to corporate climate decisions.
- Explains measurement, verification, co-benefits, scale, and buyer value.
- Uses clear outcome categories and proof.

**What Terra Nexus should adapt—not copy**

- Explain how evidence requirements depend on the intended buyer, claim, accounting treatment, or market.
- Show that soil carbon, water, biodiversity, resilience, and rural economics may be relevant together.
- Keep corporate and producer value propositions connected.

### Cultivo — Landscape and Grassland Regeneration

**What the pages do well**

- Frame land regeneration as the intersection of nature, resilience, livelihoods, technology, and capital.
- Explain why grasslands matter as ecological and economic systems.
- Connect project development with finance, carbon, biodiversity, monitoring, and landholder value.
- Use primers to educate visitors before presenting a commercial solution.

**What Terra Nexus should adapt—not copy**

- Treat land, production, communities, evidence, capital, and markets as one system.
- Use topic pages to teach enough context that the visitor understands why integration is difficult.
- Explain multiple forms of value rather than a single environmental outcome.

**What does not transfer directly**

- Cultivo positions as a nature-project and investment platform.
- Terra Nexus should remain an innovation and insights studio and should not imply a proprietary global project pipeline or investment product.

## Authoritative technical and framework sources

### USDA NRCS — Soil Health

NRCS defines soil health as the continued capacity of soil to function as a vital living ecosystem that sustains plants, animals, and humans. Its core management principles emphasize living roots, minimizing disturbance, soil cover, and biodiversity.

**Website use**

- Supports the educational explanation of soil function and common management principles.
- Supports explaining practices as components of a system rather than universal prescriptions.

### USDA NRCS — Grazing Management

NRCS describes prescribed grazing as managing the harvest of vegetation with grazing or browsing animals to achieve specific ecological, economic, and management goals. Current guidance emphasizes local conservation planning rather than treating the national standard as a standalone prescription.

**Website use**

- Supports presenting grazing as adaptive management tied to ecological and economic goals.
- Supports the need for local expertise and site-specific planning.

### SAI Platform — Regenerating Together

SAI Platform positions regenerative agriculture as an outcome-based, locally relevant approach. Its implementation structure includes context analysis, outcome selection, practice adoption, monitoring, continuous improvement, verification, and claims guidance.

**Website use**

- Supports Terra Nexus's emphasis on local context and outcomes.
- Supports explaining why implementation, monitoring, verification, and claims must be designed together.
- Supports farmer livelihoods as a foundational part of a credible program.

### WBCSD — Regenerative Agriculture Outcomes

WBCSD organizes regenerative outcomes across climate, soil, water, biodiversity, environmental integrity, financial benefits, and social benefits, with associated indicators and metrics.

**Website use**

- Supports a holistic outcome structure rather than a carbon-only page.
- Supports connecting farm and landscape outcomes with corporate reporting and value-chain decisions.

### FAO-hosted definition review

The reviewed literature and practitioner sources did not show one legal, regulatory, or widely accepted universal definition. Definitions varied between practices, outcomes, and combinations of the two.

**Website use**

- Supports the Terra Nexus guardrail against prescribing one universal definition or practice list.
- Supports transparent explanation of practice-based, outcome-based, and hybrid programs.

## Terra Nexus differentiation to preserve

Terra Nexus should not attempt to out-market a developer on its specific project, a trader on its proprietary producer program, or a platform on its software features.

The Terra Nexus page should show how clients are supported across:

- opportunity and market assessment;
- producer and participant economics;
- program and operating-model design;
- sourcing, origination, and supply-chain integration;
- technology, model, methodology, and partner diligence;
- data, MRV, traceability, and chain-of-custody design;
- environmental accounting and claims;
- commercialization and value allocation;
- launch, scaling, and managed program operations;
- verification and audit readiness.

## Content pattern for remaining topics

For each Area of Expertise, identify:

1. the production, product, supply-chain, or recovery system being changed;
2. the categories of better performance;
3. the participant groups that create or use the value;
4. the operating sequence required to move from opportunity to implementation;
5. the evidence, traceability, or assurance system;
6. potential claims and commercial pathways;
7. common program failure points;
8. specific Terra Nexus workstreams;
9. appropriate Advise and Manage applications;
10. a tailored CTA.

## Publication guardrails

- Peer websites are market examples, not neutral proof of their own claims.
- Do not reuse peer metrics, customer names, program performance, or proprietary process language.
- Verify all time-sensitive standards, program versions, and market claims before publication.
- Use peer pages to improve clarity and audience orientation—not to imitate their business model or brand.
