# Terra Nexus Areas of Expertise — Full-Depth Page Standard

**Status:** Draft content-development standard  
**Date:** August 4, 2026  
**Purpose:** Expand the nine Area of Expertise pages from short topic summaries into useful, client-facing pages without turning them into generic educational articles or publishing proprietary advisory methods.

## The job of an Area of Expertise page

Each page should help a visitor answer five questions:

1. **What is this topic and what does it include?**
2. **Why does it matter commercially to food and agribusiness companies?**
3. **What decisions must different value-chain participants resolve?**
4. **How does Terra Nexus help move from ambition to implementation and value?**
5. **What is the appropriate next conversation?**

The page should be educational enough to establish credibility, but commercial enough to explain why Terra Nexus is useful.

## Relationship to the rest of the website

- **Expertise** explains the domain in which performance or value can improve.
- **Capabilities** explain what Terra Nexus can do.
- **Who We Work With** explains which participants own or influence the relevant decisions.
- **Case Studies** provide approved evidence.

An Expertise page should connect these dimensions without duplicating the full capability or audience pages.

## Recommended page architecture

### 1. Hero

- Area-of-Expertise eyebrow
- benefit-led headline
- concise supporting statement
- one primary CTA

The hero should name the client outcome, not merely define the topic.

### 2. Topic orientation

Explain:

- what the topic includes;
- what it does not include;
- why the topic requires a systems perspective;
- why one universal intervention or practice list is usually inappropriate.

Use plain language. Avoid beginning with regulations, acronyms, or long market statistics.

### 3. What better performance can look like

Describe the primary categories of potential improvement. Depending on the topic, these may include:

- productivity and operating performance;
- producer or supplier economics;
- soil, water, biodiversity, climate, or ecosystem outcomes;
- resilience and supply security;
- product quality or differentiation;
- reduced cost, waste, volatility, or risk;
- access to customers, programs, incentives, claims, or environmental markets.

Do not guarantee that every intervention produces every outcome.

### 4. How the system works

Give the visitor a high-level operating model for the topic. This is not the proprietary Cultivation-to-Claim decision model. It is an understandable public sequence such as:

1. define the objective and boundary;
2. understand local or operational context;
3. select fit-for-purpose changes;
4. align participant economics and responsibilities;
5. establish data, evidence, traceability, and controls;
6. implement and manage the program;
7. connect verified performance to the appropriate commercial or reporting pathway.

The sequence may be adapted by topic. Do not force all topics into a farm-to-claim stage model.

### 5. Decisions clients need to resolve

Group decisions by the people who own them, for example:

- P&L, strategy, and commercial leaders;
- sourcing, origination, procurement, and operations;
- sustainability, climate, and reporting;
- program, technology, and data teams;
- finance, investment, and risk;
- producers, suppliers, or land managers.

The questions should demonstrate Terra Nexus's understanding of the real operating problem.

### 6. How Terra Nexus helps

Use client-facing workstreams rather than a bare list of the five capability families. A typical structure is:

- **Evaluate the opportunity** — market, strategy, business case, diligence, and prioritization.
- **Design the program** — operating model, participants, incentives, evidence, controls, and commercialization.
- **Build the infrastructure** — workflows, data, technology, traceability, contracts, ledgers, and governance.
- **Launch and scale** — pilots, partner selection, enrollment, implementation, change management, and expansion.
- **Manage delivery** — recurring coordination, data review, exceptions, reporting, audit readiness, and improvement.
- **Connect performance to value** — products, sourcing claims, Scope 3, certifications, credits, incentives, customer programs, or resilience value.

Map these workstreams to the five capabilities in the page design or related-content modules.

### 7. Audience pathways

Where helpful, include concise pathways for the primary audiences. Each should answer:

- what role the audience plays;
- what decisions Terra Nexus supports;
- what value or risk the audience is trying to manage.

Do not create separate audience pages unless the site architecture later requires them.

### 8. Evidence, claims, and market pathways

Explain the categories of value that improved performance may support. Clearly state that:

- pathways have different evidence and assurance requirements;
- ownership and contractual rights matter;
- some claims are chain-bound and others may be transferable;
- claims may conflict or require allocation;
- the highest nominal price is not necessarily the highest realizable shared value.

Do not publish detailed stacking rules, pricing models, or client-specific optimization logic.

### 9. Common reasons programs struggle

A short diagnostic section may include issues such as:

- unclear business objective;
- weak participant economics;
- practices selected without local context;
- evidence requirements defined too late;
- technology selected before the operating model;
- traceability that does not support the intended claim;
- misaligned contracts or attribute ownership;
- underestimated implementation and assurance costs;
- lack of recurring program management.

This section should demonstrate experience without implying a named engagement.

### 10. Advise and Manage

Explain how Terra Nexus engages:

- **Advise:** evaluate, diligence, design, model, select, and prepare.
- **Manage:** coordinate and operate recurring workstreams within a client-owned program.

Operate remains omitted unless a specific approved Terra Nexus-owned program exists.

### 11. Frameworks and standards

Use frameworks selectively. The main page should not become a directory of acronyms.

- Name only frameworks relevant to client decisions.
- Avoid static procedural detail on time-sensitive standards.
- Verify current versions and status immediately before public production.
- Do not imply universal applicability or accreditation.

### 12. Tailored CTA and advisory boundary

The CTA should fit the topic and likely buying conversation.

The advisory boundary should clarify that Terra Nexus provides strategic, commercial, technical, diligence, program-management, and verification-readiness support while working with qualified specialists where agronomic, ecological, engineering, legal, tax, accounting, certification, investment, or accredited assurance expertise is required.

## Recommended depth

A full Area of Expertise page will typically contain:

- 1,600–2,800 words of public copy;
- 7–11 primary content sections;
- 3–6 audience or outcome modules;
- 5–8 high-level Terra Nexus workstreams;
- one primary CTA and no more than one secondary CTA;
- optional approved proof and insight modules.

Pages should not be padded to reach a word count. The depth should come from clearer decisions, operating requirements, and examples of support.

## Research and source hierarchy

Use sources for different purposes:

1. **Official and primary sources** — definitions, standards, frameworks, government programs, and technical principles.
2. **Peer-reviewed research and authoritative synthesis** — environmental or production outcomes and uncertainty.
3. **Industry frameworks** — implementation models, outcome categories, verification, and claims architecture.
4. **Developer and corporate pages** — audience language, program journeys, value propositions, and examples of how market participants explain the topic.
5. **Terra Nexus records** — services, experience themes, brand positioning, audience relationships, and publication boundaries.

Developer and corporate pages are useful for understanding market communication, but their claims should not automatically be adopted as neutral facts.

## Editorial rules

### Do

- explain the topic in the context of commercial food and agribusiness decisions;
- use outcome categories and practical examples;
- emphasize local context and fit-for-purpose design;
- show the relationship among producers, traders, processors, buyers, technology, capital, and assurance;
- make Terra Nexus's role visible throughout the page;
- distinguish program design from implementation and recurring management;
- explain that evidence and claims must be designed together.

### Do not

- turn the page into a generic encyclopedia entry;
- imply that regenerative agriculture or another topic has one universal definition;
- publish a universal practice prescription;
- copy another company's program journey, claims, or brand language;
- make unqualified environmental or financial promises;
- treat carbon as the only source of value;
- present Terra Nexus as an agronomy platform, certification body, registry, accredited verifier, or project developer unless an approved record supports that role;
- display internal labels such as “decision owners,” “offering modules,” or “content architecture” as public headings;
- expose detailed intervention libraries, pricing, stacking logic, or optimization methods.
