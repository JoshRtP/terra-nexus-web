## A Sustainable Supply Chain Is an Operating System

A sustainable supply chain is not a supplier questionnaire, a software platform,
a certification logo, or a reporting dashboard.

It is the system through which products, data, money, responsibilities,
environmental attributes, and claims move among producers, originators,
processors, manufacturers, brands, retailers, technology providers, assurance
organizations, and customers.

A program can have strong upstream outcomes and still fail commercially if the
commodity is not sourced reliably, the evidence does not follow the product, the
contract does not allocate the attribute, the processing system cannot preserve
the claim, or the buyer cannot use the result.

Terra Nexus helps clients connect responsible production with the commercial and
operating infrastructure required to deliver, substantiate, and capture value.

disclosure.

Supply concentration, climate and nature exposure, disruption, fraud, human

## What Better Supply-Chain Performance Can Look Like

Potential improvements may include:

### More Reliable and Resilient Supply

- greater visibility into sourcing regions, suppliers, and critical dependencies;
- reduced exposure to concentrated, fragile, or high-risk supply;
- stronger supplier relationships and contingency planning;
- improved ability to respond to climate, market, logistics, or policy change;
- more stable quality, availability, and service.

### Better Supplier and Producer Economics

- clearer participation requirements and incentives;
- improved alignment between buyer value and supplier cost;
- more durable contracts and commercial relationships;
- reduced administrative burden and duplicated data requests;
- better access to technical support, finance, programs, or markets.

### Improved Operational Performance

- reduced waste, inventory, delay, error, or rework;
- improved planning, logistics, processing, and utilization;
- better integration of procurement, sustainability, quality, and operations;
- stronger data quality and exception management;
- more efficient program delivery at scale.

### Credible Evidence and Claims

- traceability proportionate to the intended decision;
- clear product and attribute ownership;
- consistent calculation and allocation boundaries;
- documentation that can withstand customer, auditor, certification, or
  regulatory scrutiny;
- reduced risk of double counting, unsupported claims, fraud, or reconciliation gaps.

### Stronger Commercial Value

- differentiated products, ingredients, feedstocks, or customer programs;
- access to preferred-supplier, certification, regulatory, or procurement pathways;
- improved margin, customer retention, market access, or strategic positioning;
- monetization or use of environmental attributes;
- improved resilience, supply security, and enterprise risk management.

These outcomes require trade-offs. More traceability may increase cost. More
segregation may reduce flexibility. A highly rigorous claim may apply to only a
small portion of supply. Terra Nexus helps clients determine the level of system
and evidence that fits the business objective.

## Why Traceability Must Be Designed Around the Intended Use

Traceability is not one thing.

A company may need to know the country of origin, the supplying facility, the
farm, the field, the production practice, the product batch, the environmental
attribute, or only the aggregate performance of a defined supply shed.

The right approach depends on:

- the product and supply-chain structure;
- the business decision or claim;
- the point at which materials are mixed or transformed;
- the ability to segregate, identify, or reconcile volume;
- the cost and reliability of data collection;
- the applicable certification, accounting, regulatory, or customer requirement;
- the risk of fraud, duplication, or unsupported transfer;
- whether the claim must remain physically linked to the product.

Potential chain-of-custody models may involve identity preservation,
segregation, controlled blending, mass balance, certificate-based approaches, or
other defined systems. Each has different implications for cost, flexibility,
control, and claim language.

Terra Nexus helps clients select and govern the level of traceability required
for the intended use rather than defaulting to the most complex or least costly
option.

## How a Sustainable Supply-Chain Program Moves from Sourcing to Value

### 1. Define the Business Objective and Claim Boundary

The starting objective may be responsible sourcing, supplier risk, Scope 3,
certification, a low-carbon product, regulatory eligibility, resilience,
customer reporting, product differentiation, or cost and service improvement.

Terra Nexus helps define the product, geography, supplier population, value-chain
boundary, intended outcome, user of the evidence, and level of assurance.

### 2. Map the Physical, Commercial, and Data Flows

A useful supply-chain map shows more than where the product moves. It also shows:

- who owns the material at each stage;
- where it is stored, mixed, transformed, or allocated;
- who controls producer and supplier relationships;
- which contracts govern product and attributes;
- where data are created, changed, or lost;
- which systems and teams use the information;
- where costs, value, and risk accumulate;
- which participants must cooperate for the intended outcome.

Terra Nexus helps clients identify the true control points and gaps that determine
whether a program can operate.

### 3. Design the Supplier and Producer Proposition

Suppliers and producers need clear requirements, economics, support, and value.

A program may involve premiums, cost sharing, preferred status, technical
support, long-term commitments, data payments, financing, outcome payments,
product access, or other incentives.

Terra Nexus helps structure eligibility, enrollment, evidence, contracts,
incentives, escalation, corrective action, and the treatment of existing
suppliers, new suppliers, incomplete data, and nonconforming volume.

### 4. Build Traceability, Chain of Custody, and Attribute Controls

The program must maintain a supportable relationship among physical product,
transactions, data, calculations, and claims.

Terra Nexus helps define:

- product and attribute identifiers;
- eligible-volume rules;
- segregation, commingling, and mass-balance controls;
- transaction and inventory records;
- conversion and allocation factors;
- ledger and reconciliation processes;
- certificate or claim transfer;
- exception, correction, expiry, and reversal treatment;
- documentation retention and audit trails;
- roles, approvals, and segregation of duties.

### 5. Connect Systems and Recurring Operations

Technology should support the operating model—not define it.

Relevant systems may include procurement, ERP, farm management, traceability,
LCA, carbon accounting, supplier management, quality, logistics, certification,
customer reporting, and data warehouses.

Terra Nexus helps clients define data architecture, integration, validation,
version control, workflows, roles, quality checks, and exception management. We
also help determine what should remain manual during a pilot and what should be
automated at scale.

### 6. Launch, Reconcile, and Improve

Commercial programs require recurring work across procurement, operations,
sustainability, finance, legal, technology, suppliers, customers, and assurance.

Terra Nexus can support or manage supplier onboarding, evidence review,
calculations, eligible-volume tracking, reconciliations, ledger management,
partner coordination, corrective action, customer documentation, reporting,
and audit readiness.

### 7. Connect Performance to the Right Commercial Pathway

Potential pathways may include:

- responsible-sourcing and supplier-program claims;
- certification and chain-of-custody claims;
- Scope 3 reductions and insets;
- product footprints and product differentiation;
- low-carbon feedstock or regulatory value;
- environmental attributes or credits;
- procurement qualification and customer reporting;
- resilience, service, risk, and supply-security value.

The same underlying performance may create different value for different
participants. Terra Nexus helps compare ownership, evidence, cost, market
capacity, utilization, timing, acceptance risk, and participant economics.

## Commodity Traders Are Often the Integration Point

Commodity traders and originators may connect producers, storage, aggregation,
logistics, processors, buyers, environmental attributes, and program data.

They can play several roles:

- source and aggregate eligible commodities;
- design or administer producer programs;
- maintain delivery and transaction records;
- manage physical and attribute inventories;
- translate downstream requirements into upstream workflows;
- structure customer offerings and value sharing;
- coordinate technology, agronomy, assurance, and program partners;
- manage reconciliation and commercial risk.

Terra Nexus helps traders determine which role to play, where value is created,
what controls are needed, and how to build an offering that can scale across
producers, locations, commodities, and customers.

## Decisions Clients Need to Resolve

- Which products, suppliers, sourcing regions, and impacts should be prioritized?
- What level of traceability is required for the decision or claim?
- Which chain-of-custody model fits the product and market?
- How should supplier costs, premiums, incentives, and value be allocated?
- Which data must originate upstream and which can be calculated downstream?
- How will materials, attributes, and claims be reconciled through processing?
- Who owns each attribute and who may use it?
- How should procurement, sustainability, operations, finance, technology, legal,
  and sales share accountability?
- What evidence and controls are proportionate to the commercial value?
- How will the program manage exceptions, unsupported supply, and changing requirements?

## Common Reasons Sustainable Supply-Chain Programs Struggle

- the claim is selected before the physical and commercial flow is understood;
- suppliers receive requirements without a viable economic proposition;
- traceability is more complex than the value it protects;
- chain-of-custody rules do not match actual storage, mixing, or processing;
- technology is purchased before roles and workflows are defined;
- product, inventory, carbon, certification, and financial systems use different boundaries;
- contracts do not clearly allocate attributes, representations, or liabilities;
- processing conversion and co-product allocation are poorly controlled;
- reconciliation and exception management are underestimated;
- the pilot is designed but recurring operations are not staffed or governed.

Terra Nexus helps clients address these issues before they become commercial,
claims, customer, or assurance failures.
