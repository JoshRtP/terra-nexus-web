## Regenerative Agriculture Has to Work for the Whole Value Chain

Regenerative agriculture begins with decisions made on the farm, but it scales
through the relationships, systems, incentives, and markets surrounding the farm.

A successful program must work for producers implementing change, originators
aggregating commodities, buyers funding and purchasing outcomes, technology
providers collecting and analyzing evidence, and companies accounting for and
communicating results.

Terra Nexus helps clients connect those moving parts into programs that are
commercially relevant, operationally executable, measurable, and ready for
independent review.

## Why Action Is Accelerating

The rules surrounding agricultural emissions, removals, targets, programs, and
claims are becoming more structured.

The GHG Protocol published its Land Sector and Removals Standard in 2026, with
an effective date of January 1, 2027, and released detailed implementation
guidance in June 2026.[S1][S2] The Science Based Targets initiative updated its
FLAG Guidance to Version 1.2 in March 2026, applying the revised requirements to
companies submitting targets in 2026 or later.[S3]

At the same time, government programs, carbon methodologies, industry
frameworks, certifications, and technology platforms continue to expand and
change.[S4][S5][S6][S7]

The opportunity is growing, but so is the need to align agronomy, producer
economics, sourcing, data, environmental accounting, contracts, and claims.

## Commodity Traders Can Connect Farm Performance to Market Value

Commodity traders and originators often sit at the center of regenerative
agriculture execution.

They maintain producer and supplier relationships, understand local production
systems, aggregate physical commodities, manage commercial contracts, and
connect supply with downstream customers. They may also coordinate field
evidence, environmental attributes, traceability, and program reporting.

Terra Nexus helps traders determine where they should participate and build the

## From performance improvement to market value

Improving environmental or operational performance does not automatically create
commercial value.

The same change may support several different forms of value: participation in a
program, certification, a product or marketing claim, a Scope 3 reduction, an
inset, a tradable environmental asset, a regulatory incentive, customer
qualification, corporate reporting, or greater resilience and supply security.

Those pathways do not have the same evidence requirements, operating cost,
commercial reach, ownership rules, risk, or value for each participant.

Terra Nexus helps clients connect practical improvements with the evidence,
collaboration, operating model, and market mechanisms needed to create the
highest-value supportable outcome across the value chain. Detailed intervention
menus and commercialization models remain part of client-specific work.

## Designed Around Local Production Systems

Regenerative agriculture does not have one universal practice list.

The right approach depends on the crop, climate, soil, rotation, water system,
producer objectives, market, and program requirements. A practice may create
value in one production system and introduce risk or limited benefit in another.

Terra Nexus helps clients define a program boundary that is specific enough to
be credible and flexible enough to support diverse producers and geographies.

## Decisions Clients Need to Resolve

- What business objective should the program serve?
- Which producers, crops, geographies, and interventions should be prioritized?
- Should participation be practice-based, outcome-based, or hybrid?
- How will producers be compensated and who funds the transition?
- What data must be collected directly, modeled, or independently verified?
- How will existing adopters and additionality requirements be handled?
- How will physical commodities and environmental attributes be traced and allocated?
- Which standards, methodologies, certifications, or accounting frameworks apply?
- Which claims can be made and by whom?
- How will the program remain manageable as participation grows?

## Potential claim and value pathways

- regenerative sourcing or participation claims
- certification and product-differentiation claims
- Scope 3 reductions and insets
- carbon or ecosystem-service credits
- low-carbon feedstock or regulatory value
- procurement qualification and customer reporting
- resilience, supply-security, and risk-reduction value
The right pathway depends on who can use the claim, who owns the relevant
attribute, what portion of the opportunity can be commercialized, which claims
may coexist, and whether the additional value justifies the evidence,
operating, assurance, and transaction requirements.

## Diligence That Connects Field Reality to Commercial Value

Regenerative agriculture programs can fail when one part of the system is
evaluated in isolation.

A strong practice design may lack a viable producer incentive. A sophisticated
model may rely on incomplete field data. A traceability system may not support
the intended environmental claim. A premium may not cover the cost of program
delivery. A corporate accounting approach may not match the contractual rights
or physical supply chain.

Terra Nexus evaluates the complete system, including:

- agronomic and production-system assumptions;
- producer participation and evidence;
- field data, remote sensing, and model outputs;
- calculation logic, uncertainty, and versioning;
- physical commodity and environmental-attribute controls;
- commercial contracts and value allocation;
- program governance and segregation of duties;
- fraud, anomaly, duplication, and unsupported-claim risks;
- accounting, reporting, and verification readiness.
