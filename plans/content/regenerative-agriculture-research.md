## 1. Research conclusions

### 1.1 The market is moving from broad ambition toward formal accounting and implementation rules

The GHG Protocol published the Land Sector and Removals (LSR) Standard in
January 2026 and identifies Version 1.1 as the current version. The standard is
effective January 1, 2027. Its scope includes land management, land-use change,
land-based CO2 removals, and related value-chain accounting. Detailed LSR
Guidance was released June 30, 2026 and includes dedicated chapters on spatial
boundaries and traceability, data and methods, land-management emissions,
removals, actions, targets, credited outcomes, assurance, and reporting.

**Website implication:** Regenerative agriculture can no longer be positioned
only as a practice-adoption story. Corporate programs increasingly need
traceability, inventory boundaries, data methods, accounting controls, and
assurance readiness.

### 1.2 Target-setting requirements are also changing

The Science Based Targets initiative released FLAG Guidance Version 1.2 on
March 19, 2026. SBTi states that Version 1.2 applies immediately and that
companies submitting targets in 2026 or later must align with the updated
requirements. SBTi requires FLAG targets for specified land-intensive sectors
and for other companies whose FLAG-related emissions meet its stated threshold.

**Website implication:** Food, agriculture, processing, and retail companies
need programs capable of connecting interventions and supply-chain evidence to
target-setting and progress reporting.

### 1.3 Government funding and conservation delivery remain important, but program details are time-sensitive

USDA NRCS launched a Regenerative Pilot Program in December 2025. The agency
describes it as a farmer-first, outcomes-based conservation approach and states
that it is investing $700 million through EQIP and CSP. NRCS also continues to
operate EQIP, CSP, and RCPP.

**Website implication:** Terra Nexus should discuss public-private incentive
coordination and program stacking at a high level, but all funding, eligibility,
and application statements must be rechecked before publication.

### 1.4 Environmental-market methodologies continue to evolve

Verra lists VM0042 Improved Agricultural Land Management v2.2 as active since
October 21, 2025. Verra issued corrections and clarifications effective June 11,
2026, and a major v3.0 revision is in independent expert review.

Climate Action Reserve lists US Soil Enrichment Protocol v1.1 as available for
project submission and under revision, with October 2025 errata and clarifications.

BCarbon published Soil Carbon Protocol v3.0 in February 2026.

ACR maintains agricultural and land methodologies, but several historical
agricultural methodologies are inactive. Any public reference to a specific ACR
methodology must be checked for current status and eligibility.

**Website implication:** The public page should list selected frameworks as
experience references, not provide static procedural summaries or imply that
every methodology remains active.

### 1.5 Regenerative agriculture frameworks are proliferating rather than converging on one universal definition

The SAI Platform launched its Regenerating Together Programme in June 2026,
describing a globally aligned but adaptable framework across crops, dairy, and
beef. Rainforest Alliance's Regenerative Agriculture Standard became effective
March 1, 2026. Other frameworks include Regenerative Organic Certified,
Regenified, Land to Market, and ISCC-linked agricultural systems.

Rainforest Alliance states that its development review found no harmonized or
official definition of regenerative agriculture.

**Website implication:** Terra Nexus's approved guardrail—not prescribing one
universal definition or practice list—is well supported. The page should focus
on fit-for-purpose program architecture, local context, outcomes, evidence, and
claims.

## 2. Peer positioning review

The peer market clusters into several models.

### A. Enterprise MRV and Scope 3 platforms

**Regrow** positions around field-level insight, scenario planning, grower
enrollment, MRV, and Scope 3 reporting for food, fiber, beverage, beef, and
biofuel supply chains.

**CIBO / Terion** positions around program deployment, scientific modeling, MRV,
grower engagement, agricultural analytics, program-management software, and
enterprise system integration.

**Implication for Terra Nexus:** Do not compete as another generic MRV platform.
Position Terra Nexus as the independent party that evaluates technology,
connects tools to commercial and operating requirements, and manages the spaces
between systems and organizations.

### B. Farmer-transition and payment platforms

**Klim** emphasizes farmer guidance, regenerative practice adoption, soil
health, carbon outcomes, and direct financial rewards.

**Soil and Water Outcomes Fund** emphasizes outcomes-based payments, farmer
enrollment, public-private partnerships, carbon and water outcomes, and field
verification.

**Implication for Terra Nexus:** Differentiate through program strategy,
origination, business-model design, diligence, operating controls, and
client-owned program management rather than farmer enrollment alone.

### C. Agronomy and origination networks

**Truterra** emphasizes a farmer-driven network, local advisors, field access,
and downstream supply-chain sustainability programs.

**Implication for Terra Nexus:** Highlight independence across originators,
buyers, technology providers, agronomists, accounting frameworks, and verifiers.
Commodity traders are a primary audience, but Terra Nexus is not positioned as
the physical originator unless specifically engaged in that role.

### D. Certifications and assessment systems

SAI Platform, Rainforest Alliance, Regenerative Organic Certified, Regenified,
Land to Market, and ISCC provide different combinations of practices, outcomes,
verification, product claims, and chain-of-custody requirements.

**Implication for Terra Nexus:** Position certification and framework selection
as a client decision that depends on product, geography, market, desired claim,
supply-chain structure, and evidence—not as a universal recommendation.

## 3. Terra Nexus differentiation

Recommended differentiation statement:

> Terra Nexus connects the commercial, agricultural, data, environmental,
> operational, and assurance decisions required to move regenerative agriculture
> from cultivation to credible market claims.

Supporting differentiators:

- commodity origination and agricultural supply-chain understanding;
- producer-program and operating-model design;
- environmental-market and corporate-accounting fluency;
- technology, model, and data diligence;
- commercial economics and value-allocation modeling;
- traceability and chain-of-custody controls;
- verification and audit readiness;
- managed execution within client-owned programs;
- independence from any single software platform, certification, methodology,
  input product, or crediting program.

## 5. Positioning guardrails

Do not claim that Terra Nexus:

- defines regenerative agriculture for the market;
- guarantees soil-carbon gains, emissions reductions, yields, resilience, or profitability;
- guarantees Scope 3 recognition, target acceptance, certification, or credit issuance;
- performs accredited validation or verification;
- provides licensed agronomic prescriptions in every geography;
- replaces the applicable standard, registry, certification body, or government program;
- guarantees that modeled or remotely sensed outcomes will be accepted;
- represents illustrative companies as clients.
