## Core distinction

- **Value-chain node:** where activity occurs and the corresponding Who We Work With audience.
- **Area of Expertise:** the domain in which performance, resilience, sustainability, or commercial outcomes can be improved.
- **Intervention:** a fit-for-purpose change to practices, products, processes, systems, data, or collaboration.
- **Claim:** Terra Nexus's umbrella term for the ways improved performance creates value, whether public or internal, chain-bound or transferable, financial or nonfinancial.
- **Cultivation-to-Claim play:** the integrated pathway connecting improved performance to the highest-value supportable claim or combination of claims across the participants required to deliver it.

## Shared-value principle

The highest-value pathway is not determined by selecting the claim with the
highest nominal unit price.

Terra Nexus evaluates how value is created and used by each participant. A
producer may receive an incentive or margin uplift; a trader may create
origination or program value; a food company may advance Scope 3 targets or
differentiate a product; a retailer may strengthen assortment and customer
value; and another buyer may purchase a transferable environmental asset.

The relevant comparison includes:

- participant-specific revenue, margin, cost, risk, and strategic value;
- claim ownership and contractual rights;
- evidence, MRV, traceability, assurance, and operating requirements;
- time to market, durability, scalability, and acceptance risk;
- the portion of the opportunity that can actually be commercialized;
- whether claims can coexist or must remain exclusive;
- double-counting, attribute-allocation, and stacking constraints; and
- the ability to unlock shared value across the complete play.

## Public-website boundary

The website should explain that Terra Nexus connects performance improvement to
commercial value. It should not publish:

- exhaustive intervention libraries;
- ranch-, farm-, facility-, or product-specific recommendations;
- full claim-stacking decision trees;
- pricing databases or value-allocation models;
- client-specific playbooks; or
- proprietary optimization logic.
