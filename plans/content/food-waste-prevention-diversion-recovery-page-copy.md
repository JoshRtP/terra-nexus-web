## What This Topic Includes

The strategies, operating systems, markets, technologies, and partnerships used to prevent food loss and waste, keep edible food in use, create value from byproducts, and recover remaining resources.

Covers prevention, forecasting, inventory and process improvement, secondary markets, donation and rescue, upcycling, animal feed and ingredient pathways, organics recycling, composting, anaerobic digestion, resource recovery, waste-to-value business models, measurement, and claims. The hierarchy should prioritize prevention and highest-value use before lower-value recovery.

## From performance improvement to market value

Improving environmental or operational performance does not automatically create
commercial value.

The same change may support several different forms of value: participation in a
program, certification, a product or marketing claim, a Scope 3 reduction, an
inset, a tradable environmental asset, a regulatory incentive, customer
qualification, corporate reporting, or greater resilience and supply security.

Those pathways do not have the same evidence requirements, operating cost,
commercial reach, ownership rules, risk, or value for each participant.

Terra Nexus helps clients connect practical improvements with the evidence,
collaboration, operating model, and market mechanisms needed to create the
highest-value supportable outcome across the value chain. Detailed intervention
menus and commercialization models remain part of client-specific work.

## Decisions Clients Need to Resolve

- Where is food or material value being lost, and what are the root causes?
- Which opportunities should be prevented, redistributed, repurposed, recycled, or recovered?
- What food-safety, quality, liability, logistics, and market constraints apply?
- Which secondary products, ingredients, feed, or energy pathways have viable demand?
- How should avoided cost, new revenue, capital, and partner economics be modeled?
- Which technologies and service providers fit the operating environment?
- How should waste, loss, diversion, emissions, and circularity claims be measured?
- How will the program scale across sites without shifting impacts elsewhere?

## Potential claim and value pathways

- waste-prevention, diversion, and circularity claims
- corporate reporting and target progress
- upcycled-product and ingredient differentiation
- avoided-cost and new-revenue value
- regulatory or landfill-diversion value
- emissions-reduction and resource-recovery value
- resilience and operational-efficiency value

The right pathway depends on who can use the claim, who owns the relevant
attribute, what portion of the opportunity can be commercialized, which claims
may coexist, and whether the additional value justifies the evidence,
operating, assurance, and transaction requirements.
