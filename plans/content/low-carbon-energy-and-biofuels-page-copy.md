## Low-Carbon Fuel Value Begins Before the Feedstock Reaches the Plant

A biofuel facility may control its process energy, yields, coproduct treatment,
utilities, and operating performance. A significant portion of the fuel pathway,
however, may begin upstream with how the feedstock is produced, documented,
procured, aggregated, and transferred.

A favorable field or feedstock carbon-intensity result does not create value by
itself. The feedstock must be commercially sourced, the supporting evidence must
be complete, the physical volume and environmental characteristics must be
controlled, the calculation must connect correctly to the fuel pathway, and the
result must support the intended regulatory, tax, customer, or market use.

Terra Nexus helps clients connect agricultural production, commodity
origination, lifecycle analysis, contracts, controls, economics, and assurance
into one field-to-fuel operating system.

## Why Field-Level Analysis Is Only the Beginning

Agricultural carbon-intensity tools can help estimate how location, yield,
fertilizer, energy, and eligible management affect feedstock results. But a
commercial program must answer a broader set of questions:

- Is the data accurate, complete, current, and attributable to the feedstock?
- Which records support each input?
- Can the feedstock be sourced at meaningful volume?
- How much supply is traceable and verifiable?
- How are conventional and reduced-CI volumes handled?
- Which party makes each representation and retains each record?
- How is the feedstock characteristic transferred to the facility?
- How does feedstock CI connect to the applicable lifecycle pathway?
- What portion of projected value can actually be claimed or transacted?
- Does the available value justify producer payments and program cost?
- Can the complete system withstand tax, customer, investor, auditor, or verifier review?

Terra Nexus helps clients move from a calculation result to a supportable
commercial and operating program.

## The Field-to-Fuel Operating System

| Program stage | Questions the program must answer |
|---|---|
| Field and producer | Which production data and records support the feedstock result? |
| Feedstock analysis | How do geography, yield, nutrients, energy, and eligible practices affect CI? |
| Producer proposition | Why should the producer participate, and what evidence and commitments are required? |
| Origination | Where can reduced-CI volume be sourced, aggregated, contracted, and priced? |
| Delivery and aggregation | How are eligible and conventional volumes identified, stored, mixed, and reconciled? |
| Chain of custody | How are product characteristics and attributes transferred without unsupported claims or double counting? |
| Fuel pathway | How does feedstock CI interact with lifecycle modeling, facility emissions, and coproducts? |
| Commercial value | How are incentives, premiums, costs, margin, ownership, and risk allocated? |
| Program operations | Which calculations, ledgers, reviews, approvals, and exception controls recur? |
| Assurance | Can the evidence, transactions, calculations, controls, and claims withstand independent scrutiny? |

The stages are connected. A weakness at the farm or delivery location can affect
the facility claim. A contractual or ledger gap can undermine an otherwise valid
calculation. An attractive modeled value can disappear if only a small portion
of supply is supportable.

## Common Reasons Low-Carbon Feedstock Programs Struggle

- the business case assumes every sourced bushel or tonne is traceable;
- field inputs are accepted without adequate source documentation;
- feedstock CI is analyzed separately from the facility pathway and commercial volume;
- premiums are based on nominal credit value rather than realizable margin;
- producer, originator, intermediary, and facility responsibilities are unclear;
- mass-balance rules do not match actual delivery, storage, and inventory operations;
- contracts do not allocate environmental attributes and representations clearly;
- spreadsheet, platform, tax, and lifecycle-model boundaries are inconsistent;
- exceptions, corrections, version changes, and unsupported supply are not governed;
- verification is treated as a final document exercise rather than a program-design requirement;
- recurring operations are underestimated after the initial pilot.

Terra Nexus helps clients identify these gaps before value is contracted,
claimed, financed, or transferred.
