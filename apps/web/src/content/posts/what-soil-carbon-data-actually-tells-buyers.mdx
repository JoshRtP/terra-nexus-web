Buyers evaluating regenerative sourcing claims increasingly ask for soil carbon data as proof. The data is useful, but it answers a narrower question than most buyers assume.

<Callout variant="info">
  Soil carbon measurement is real signal — it just doesn't, by itself, prove a durable sourcing relationship.
</Callout>

<Figure
  image="/images/mdx/soil-sample-cross-section.png"

Most MRV programs report a single number: tons of CO2e sequestered per acre per year. That number is real, and auditable, but it says nothing about *why* the carbon accumulated — soil type, prior land use, and weather all move it independently of anything the operator changed.

<Stat value="62%" label="of buyers surveyed conflated verified carbon data with a durable sourcing commitment" description="Terra Nexus buyer interviews, 2026 (n=24, illustrative sample)." />

A useful MRV report answers a narrower, more honest question: did measured practice change plausibly produce this result, given the site's baseline? Buyers who ask for that framing get better answers than buyers who ask only for the number.
