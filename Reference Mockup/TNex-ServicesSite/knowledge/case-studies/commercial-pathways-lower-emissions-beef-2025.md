# Evaluating Commercial Pathways for Lower-Emissions Beef from Optimized Diets

## Overview

A global agricultural trading company was evaluating the commercial potential of a proprietary feed-optimization solution designed for use in US beef feedlot systems.

The company wanted to understand whether emissions reductions associated with improved feeding strategies could support credible customer-facing claims, supply-chain insets, and other forms of commercial value. It also needed to assess whether the underlying emissions methodology, market positioning, and claims structure were sufficiently developed to support further commercialization.

Terra Nexus conducted an integrated market, methodology, and program-readiness assessment. The work evaluated potential emissions-reduction pathways, reviewed the applicability of IPCC Tier 2 enteric-methane methodologies and Verra VM0041, assessed emerging market expectations, and developed recommendations for positioning the solution within lower-emissions beef supply chains.

## The challenge

Feed optimization can potentially reduce the emissions intensity of beef production through changes in animal performance, feed efficiency, and the emissions associated with producing each unit of beef. Translating those reductions into commercial value, however, requires more than demonstrating a technical improvement.

The client needed to determine:

- whether the emissions reductions could be calculated and supported using credible methodological approaches;
- which types of claims could be made by the solution provider, beef producers, and downstream customers;
- whether the reductions could contribute to supply-chain inset programs;
- how potential customers and market participants might evaluate the solution;
- how competing feed and animal-nutrition solutions were being positioned;
- what additional evidence, methodology development, or program infrastructure would be required before commercialization.

The assessment therefore needed to connect technical carbon accounting with customer expectations, claims integrity, competitive positioning, ownership of environmental attributes, and potential revenue opportunities.

## Terra Nexus’s role

Terra Nexus served as a strategic and technical advisor across five connected workstreams.

### Commercialization strategy

We evaluated how emissions reductions associated with the feed-optimization solution could be translated into differentiated customer value.

The analysis considered potential applications including:

- lower-emissions product positioning;
- supply-chain inset generation;
- emissions-reduction claims;
- customer sustainability programs;
- commercial offerings for participants in the beef value chain.

The work focused on identifying credible pathways to value while distinguishing near-term opportunities from pathways that would require additional methodological or program development.

### Market-mechanism assessment

Terra Nexus assessed the mechanisms through which emissions reductions might be recognized, transferred, or monetized.

This included evaluating the suitability of the solution for:

- product-level environmental claims;
- value-chain emissions reductions;
- customer insetting programs;
- emissions-reduction reporting;
- potential commercial differentiation.

Each mechanism was considered in relation to likely evidence requirements, ownership of environmental attributes, traceability needs, rights to claim the reductions, and the expectations of downstream customers.

### Internal methodology review

We reviewed the feed-optimization documentation and results and assessed the methodological readiness of the solution. This included reviewing the potential application of IPCC Tier 2 approaches for estimating enteric-methane emissions and evaluating the potential applicability of Verra VM0041 to a crediting project.

The review considered whether the available calculation approach could credibly support the intended market applications and where additional substantiation might be required.

The assessment did not treat methodological alignment alone as sufficient for a market claim. It also considered the relationship between the emissions model, the intervention being evaluated, the production system, and the specific claim or commercial mechanism the client intended to pursue.

### Program-readiness assessment

Terra Nexus evaluated the broader requirements for moving from a technical emissions-reduction concept to a market-ready program.

The review considered areas such as:

- methodological documentation;
- evidence and data requirements;
- attribution of emissions reductions;
- claims boundaries;
- customer acceptance;
- traceability and reporting expectations;
- internal governance;
- potential verification needs.

This allowed the client to distinguish between elements that were already sufficiently developed and those requiring further work before broader market deployment.

### Market acceptance and competitive benchmarking

We assessed how feed-based emissions solutions were being positioned within the market and how prospective customers might evaluate their credibility and value.

The work included:

- reviewing competing feed and animal-nutrition solutions;
- comparing market positioning and claims approaches;
- assessing customer-facing value propositions;
- evaluating potential market acceptance;
- identifying differentiators and commercialization risks.

The benchmarking helped place the client’s solution within the broader market for lower-emissions livestock production.

## Our approach

Terra Nexus combined technical methodology review with commercial and market analysis.

### 1. Define potential emissions-reduction applications

We first considered the ways in which feed-optimization benefits could potentially be used across the beef value chain.

These included claims made directly about the solution, emissions reductions reported by beef producers, and reductions incorporated into the supply-chain climate programs of downstream customers.

### 2. Evaluate methodological credibility

We reviewed the proposed emissions-accounting approach and its relationship to IPCC Tier 2 enteric-methane methodologies and the potential applicability of Verra VM0041.

The purpose was to determine whether the methodology was appropriate for the intended applications and to identify limitations that could affect credibility, comparability, or verification.

### 3. Assess claims pathways

We evaluated the types of claims that different value-chain participants might seek to make.

The analysis considered the distinction between:

- statements about product performance;
- claims about reduced emissions intensity;
- supply-chain emissions reductions;
- inset-related claims;
- broader environmental marketing claims.

This distinction was important because each type of claim may require different evidence, accounting boundaries, ownership provisions, and controls.

### 4. Review market expectations

We considered how prospective customers and other market participants were likely to evaluate the solution.

This included examining the importance of:

- transparent calculations;
- clear baselines;
- traceable implementation;
- defensible claims language;
- credible monitoring;
- alignment with customer climate strategies.

### 5. Benchmark competing solutions

Terra Nexus reviewed the positioning of comparable feed and animal-nutrition offerings to understand how competitors communicated emissions benefits and approached commercialization.

The comparison helped identify areas in which the client’s solution could be differentiated, as well as claims or market positions that could create credibility risks.

### 6. Develop commercialization recommendations

The findings were translated into recommendations covering potential claims structures, market positioning, and commercialization pathways.

The recommendations were designed to help the client determine which opportunities could be advanced immediately and which required additional technical, programmatic, or market development.

## Deliverables

Terra Nexus provided the client with:

- commercialization recommendations for a feed-based emissions-reduction solution;
- an assessment of potential market mechanisms;
- recommendations for customer-facing claims structures;
- a review of methodology and program readiness;
- competitive benchmarking of feed and animal-nutrition solutions;
- insights on potential market acceptance and commercial positioning.

## Outcomes

The engagement identified commercially viable pathways through which feed-optimization emissions reductions could support value creation within the beef supply chain.

It also clarified:

- the claims and inset pathways that appeared most relevant;
- the relationship between methodological readiness and commercial credibility;
- the evidence and program requirements associated with different market mechanisms;
- how the solution could be positioned relative to competing offerings;
- how rights to claim the emissions reductions could be preserved while reducing the risk of stranded environmental attributes;
- where further development would be needed before broader commercialization.

The work provided a structured basis for incorporating the emissions benefits of the solution into customer offerings, supply-chain climate programs, and lower-emissions beef strategies.

## Business value

The engagement focused on translating technically credible emissions reductions into commercial value.

The commercialization assessment modeled a pathway capable of supporting **$12.5 million per year in recurring contract value** and an **8.25% normalized margin improvement**. The client subsequently achieved those modeled commercial results.

Additional value pathways evaluated through the engagement included:

- differentiated product positioning;
- stronger customer sustainability propositions;
- participation in supply-chain inset programs;
- recognition of emissions reductions by downstream customers;
- recurring revenue associated with lower-emissions products and services.

The analysis did not assume that quantified emissions reductions would automatically create value. It identified the methodological, claims, market, ownership, and program conditions required to support a credible commercial offering.
